The problem

The answers exist. They're just buried, and generic AI makes them up.

A dealer-group CFO's reference layer lives in thousand-page OEM accounting manuals, multi-MB NADA and Kerrigan reports, and state-by-state warranty statutes — none of it searchable, all of it billable.

01

Benchmarking blind

"Are we above or below the average dealer?" is the question behind every principal conversation — and it takes an analyst an hour of PDF-digging to answer with a number you can defend.

02

No reference layer

The OEM Dealer Standard Accounting Manuals define every line on the statement. No product reads them. DMS platforms run operations; they don't cite the manual that governs the account.

03

Hallucination is disqualifying

A CFO who has been burned once by confident, wrong software won't type a second question. Generic copilots always answer. In this seat, "always answer" is the bug.

The product

Every answer carries the page that proves it — or it doesn't answer.

Margin reads the corpus, retrieves the passage, and shows it side-by-side with the source PDF. The hard rule is the product: verbatim quote and page citation, or a refusal on the record. That trust posture is the moat generic AI can't copy without rewriting its own UX.

Score the passage, not a guess

The question retrieves against the index. If a passage clears the bar, it's returned verbatim with its page number. If nothing scores, nothing is invented.
