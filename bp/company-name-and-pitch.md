## Company name

**Margin**

Reasoning, condensed:

- **Dual meaning that binds the product to its mechanism.** *Profit margin* (the subject every dealer principal watches) × *margin of the page* (where every cited quote literally appears in the source PDF). The name carries both halves of what Margin does.
- **Fits the competitive register.** The dealer-tech landscape runs short, professional, ownable names: CDK, Reynolds & Reynolds, NCM, NADA, Tekion, Numa, Flai. *Margin* sits in that company without straining.
- **One word, one syllable, easy to say.** Passes the "say it once on a phone call and have it stick" test.
- **Open verbal positioning.** "Margin tells you where you stand on margin, with the page that defines it."

Recommended after explicitly ruling out **Folio** (heavily contested in 2026 SaaS — *usefolio.ai*, *folioai.com*, *foliosi.com*, *folio3.ai*, plus Folio Investments; trademark in software classes materially harder; see `market-research.md` §"On naming, given the landscape").

**Before incorporation:**
- Formal USPTO trademark search in **Class 9** (downloadable software) and **Class 42** (SaaS).
- Domain audit. Candidates worth pricing: `margin.cfo`, `margin.so`, `getmargin.com`, `usemargin.com`, `margincopilot.com`. (None verified yet.)

## Company one-liner (≤ 250 characters)

### Recommended  &nbsp;·&nbsp; 220 chars

> **Margin is an AI copilot for dealer-group CFOs that answers every question with a verbatim quote and page citation from an OEM accounting manual or NADA benchmark — or refuses on the record when the source doesn't say so.**

What this one-liner is doing, in order:

1. **Names the buyer** — *dealer-group CFOs*. Not "dealers," not "automotive," not "finance teams." The buyer profile is the defensible part.
2. **Names the corpus** — *OEM accounting manual or NADA benchmark*. The two reference layers that define the vertical.
3. **Names the trust mechanism** — *verbatim quote and page citation … or refuses on the record*. The differentiator and the cultural moat.
4. **Spends "AI" exactly once.** Enough to signal category. Not so much that it crowds substance.

## What the one-liner deliberately does *not* say

- **No mention of Next.js, FastAPI, Elasticsearch, sentence-transformers, or Claude.** Implementation. The buyer does not care.
- **No "powered by [model]."** Same reason. Coupling the brand to a vendor is fragile.
- **No "saves time" / "10× productivity" / "transforms your workflow."** Vague claims are what every AI startup says. Margin's claim is specific and externally verifiable: every answer cites a page or refuses.
- **No "for the automotive industry."** Too broad. The product is for *dealer-group CFOs* specifically. The buyer profile is what's defensible — automotive is the world it lives in, not the audience it serves.
- **No "upload your own documents."** It is a feature. It is not the headline.

## Companion long-form  &nbsp;·&nbsp; 1-paragraph elevator, ~600 chars

For decks, founder bios, and deck-cover blurbs where the 250-char limit is not the constraint:

> Margin is an AI copilot for dealer-group CFOs. It reads the OEM Dealer Standard Accounting Manuals (GM, Ford), the industry benchmarks (NADA, Kerrigan), and the regulatory references (IRS Pub 538, NIADA, state warranty law) — plus any documents the user uploads. Every answer is a verbatim quote with a PDF page citation, side-by-side with the source, or a refusal on the record when nothing scores. The hard rule *is* the product. Multi-tenant. Per-user index. No DMS lock-in.
