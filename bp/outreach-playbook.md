# Outreach Playbook — Getting to 10–20 Discovery Interviews

**Date:** 2026-07-07
**Owner:** Alan Wu
**Goal:** Book **10–20 discovery interviews** with dealer finance people over the next 3–4 weeks to validate the problem behind *Margin* — **before** building the demo corpus or selling anything.
**Budget:** $100 (mostly unspent — see §7. Money is not the bottleneck; message quality and follow-through are.)

---

## 0. The one strategic idea

You have **no public demo and no corpus data**. That is fine, because discovery interviews don't need one. It also decides your posture:

> You are **not** a vendor showing a product. You are a **Cornell engineer learning how dealer accounting actually works**, anchored on a real, specific pain you already understand.

This is honest (it matches exactly where you are), and it's the *highest-response* posture for this audience. A busy controller will decline a demo but will happily teach a smart, curious student for 20 minutes. Your outsider status is an **asset**, not a liability — lean into it. Never fake domain authority; a dealer CFO who Googles you finds a BCI/neuroscience researcher, and "curious builder" is the only story that survives that search.

**The proof you're not clueless** — carry one concrete, real dealer-accounting insight from the Robert conversation into every message. The best one:

> *"The factory statement tells you an expense is 5% of gross — but it won't tell you **which** gross, because it might pull from a different page than the one you're looking at."*

That single sentence tells any controller you've actually been near the problem. It's your credential.

## 2. The channels & the exact scripts

Run all of these; they compound. Personalize the first line of every message — a copy-paste blast to this audience dies instantly.

### A. LinkedIn — your primary engine

**Step 1 — Connection request (≤300 chars, no pitch):**
> Hi [First] — I'm a Cornell engineer building a research tool for dealership finance teams. Not selling anything; I'm trying to understand how controllers actually reconcile the factory statement (e.g., which "gross" an expense % is pulling from). Would value learning from you if you're open.

**Step 2 — After they accept, DM:**
> Thanks for connecting, [First]. Quick context: I'm building a tool that reads the OEM accounting manuals + NADA benchmarks and answers dealer-finance questions with the exact page it came from. Before I build more of it, I'm interviewing people who live in the factory statement so I don't build the wrong thing.
>
> Could I ask you 20 minutes? I'm not pitching — I mostly want to hear where the statement lies to you and what you've had to reverse-engineer. Any weekday works; here's my calendar: [Calendly link]. And genuinely fine if not.

### B. Cold email

**Subject lines (pick per target, keep it curious not salesy):**
- `Quick question about the [Brand] factory statement`
- `Learning how controllers reconcile the factory statement`
- `Cornell engineer, 20 min, dealer accounting`

**Body:**
> Hi [First],
>
> I'm a Cornell engineer building a research tool for dealer finance teams — think "ask a question, get the exact page from the OEM accounting manual or NADA benchmark that answers it."
>
> I'm not selling anything (there's barely a product yet). I'm interviewing controllers and CFOs to understand the real problem before I build the wrong thing. The specific thing I keep hearing: the factory statement says an expense is X% of gross but won't tell you *which* gross or *which page* it pulled from — so you reverse-engineer it by hand.
>
> Would you be open to 20 minutes to tell me how you actually handle that? Grab any slot that works: [Calendly link]. Totally fine if not — thanks either way.
>
> Alan
> [alanywu.github.io]

### C. Phone (highest hit-rate with this phone-culture audience)

Call the dealership main line, ask for the **controller** or **office manager** by name if you have it.

**Gatekeeper:** "Hi, could I reach [Name], your controller? … It's Alan, I'm a student researcher — not a sales call — I'm doing interviews on dealership accounting and wanted to ask them a question."

**When you reach them:**
> Hi [Name] — thanks for picking up. I'll be quick and I'm not selling anything. I'm a Cornell engineer building a research tool for dealer finance teams, and I'm interviewing controllers to understand how you reconcile the factory statement — like figuring out which gross an expense percentage is really pulling from. Would you have 15–20 minutes sometime this week, or even a few minutes right now if it's a good time?

If yes now → run the interview (§4). If not → offer to email a calendar link.

### D. X / Twitter & communities (ASOTU, #dealertwitter, DealerRefresh)

**Do not cold-pitch.** Engage first:
1. Follow ASOTU, DealerRefresh, and 10–15 dealer-finance/#dealertwitter accounts.
2. Reply *usefully* to posts about dealer accounting, fixed-ops, or DMS pain for 1–2 weeks. Become a recognizable name.
3. Post your own insight (see §3). When someone engages, DM the same 20-minute ask.
4. DM people who *post their own pain* — warmest possible cold DM.

### E. CPA firms / 20-Group consultants (force multiplier)

Slightly warmer tone — they talk shop for a living:
> Hi [Name] — I'm a Cornell engineer building an AI research tool for dealer finance (answers questions with the exact page from the OEM manual/NADA, or refuses if the source doesn't say). You work across many dealers, so you see patterns no single controller does. Could I get 25 minutes to learn where your dealer clients most often get burned by the factory statement? Not a pitch — I'm trying to build the right thing. [Calendly]

### F. University network (Cornell/USC) — warm layer

- Post the ask in Cornell/USC entrepreneurship Slack/Discords and alumni groups: *"Building an AI tool for car-dealership CFOs. Anyone connected to auto retail or a dealer-facing CPA firm? Looking for 20-min interviews."*
- Email a professor in entrepreneurship / the eLab or blackstone launchpad; ask for one intro to auto-retail or accounting alumni.
- LinkedIn: filter your target search by "Cornell" / "USC" alumni — a shared school triples reply rates.

### G. Robert (optional, low-pressure)

A month of silence isn't a hard no. **One** soft re-touch, no ask attached:
> Hey Robert — no ask, just wanted to say the unapplied-labor thing you explained (going 20→13 techs and making the most in 12 months) has stuck with me. It's become the exact use case I'm building around. Hope the store's treating you well.

If he replies, *then* ask for 20 minutes or an intro. If not, drop it — you have other channels.

## 4. The interview itself (so the 20 minutes are worth it)

**Rules (Mom Test):**
- **Don't pitch. Don't describe Margin until the last 2 minutes**, if at all.
- **Don't ask "would you use/buy this?"** — people lie to be nice. Ask about the **past and the present**, never the hypothetical future.
- Ask about their **actual last-month workflow**, specific numbers, and real frustrations.
- Shut up and let them talk. Silence is your friend.

**Question spine:**
1. Walk me through what you did the last time you closed the monthly statement — screen by screen.
2. Where does the factory statement *lie* to you, or not tell you where a number comes from?
3. What have you had to reverse-engineer by hand? (listen for the "which gross" / cross-page problem)
4. What's the thing you check that nobody else in the store looks at? (listen for unapplied labor, etc.)
5. When you're unsure what an OEM account or policy means, what do you do — who or what do you check?
6. What would have to be true for you to trust a tool's answer on any of this?
7. Who else should I talk to? *(always ask — this is how 10 becomes 20)*

**Last 2 minutes (only if it flowed well):** "I'm building something for exactly this — can I come back and show you when it's real?" → that's your design-partner pipeline.
